## Bundle 1 — `tacacs-2960-authz-20260906`

One command, `show version` on LAB-SWITCH-1, described three times by
three different processes at **21:48:33 UTC**.

| record | where | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| `gate-enforce:LAB-SWITCH-1` seq 8 `gateintent-3f7046241cd9dac4` | chained, 313 | the gate declaring what it was about to run |
| `gate-enforce:LAB-SWITCH-1` seq 9 `gateexec-ec64292977fae4e1` | chained, 313 | the gate's signed GREEN observation of the output |
| `tacacs:virp-onode-home` seq 243 | chained, 313 | the switch's own accounting record, `cmd=show version <cr>`, `user=virp-ro` |
| `exhibits/ct215-decision-log-excerpt.txt` | **unsigned**, CT 215 | the authorization decision that permitted it |

Chain verdicts: both sessions `CRYPTOGRAPHICALLY-VERIFIED`, 10 and 248
entries, `contiguity`, `genesis`, `links`, `head_commitment`,
`entry_signatures`, `head_signature`, `session_key_binding` and
`artifact_binding` all `VERIFIED`, `signer_trust PINNED`.

### What each record proves

- **The gate observation** proves the gate held bytes matching that
  hash, under a key it does not control the verification of, in a chain
  whose sequence has no gaps. It does **not** prove the switch produced
  those bytes.
- **The accounting record** is the switch's own account of running the
  command, authenticated by a shared key at the point of collection. It
  is the only one of the three the *device* originates.
- **Together** they are hard to forge separately: the gate would have to
  fabricate output for a command the switch also independently
  accounted, at the same second, with both records landing in a chain
  whose head is signed.

## Bundle 2 — `tacacs-nhoward-session-20260906`

The human session, 21:17:47–21:18:52: login, EXEC authorization,
`configure terminal`, `show version`, `write`.

Chain verdict: `CRYPTOGRAPHICALLY-VERIFIED`, 252 entries, `signer_trust
PINNED`.

### This bundle is deliberately missing a record, and the absence is the point

**There is no gate observation in it, and there cannot be.** A human on
a VTY does not go through the gate, so nothing signs what the operator
saw. What exists is the switch's accounting of what the operator ran and
the server's decision to permit it. Nobody holds a signed copy of the
*output* the operator was shown.

That is the `UNGOVERNED` shape `docs/TACACS-ACCOUNTING.md` §9.2
describes, and it is why routing operators through TACACS+ buys
attribution rather than verification.

## What the unsigned exhibit does NOT prove

Both bundles carry `exhibits/` — the CT 215 access and authz lines for
the same timestamps, with a sha256 of the excerpt. **`virp-verify` never
reads these files.** It does not grade them and its
`CRYPTOGRAPHICALLY-VERIFIED` verdict says nothing whatever about them.

Demonstrated rather than asserted: the bundle's `manifest.json` sha256
is **`ffbcc6be…` both before and after** the exhibit was added. The
exhibit is outside the signed set by construction.

Specifically, the exhibit does **not** establish:

- **that CT 215 wrote those lines.** The sha256 binds the file to what
  was copied out at export time. Anyone who can write
  `/var/log/tacacs` can alter the log first, and **CT 215 is the same
  host that made the decisions** — the machine with the motive holds the
  only copy.
- **that the excerpt is complete.** Selected lines. A denial removed
  before extraction leaves no trace here.
- **that the decision caused the outcome.** Correlation is by timestamp
  and command text, not by any cryptographic binding between the
  decision and the gate's observation.

**This closes when Phase 2 item 3 ships.** The decision submitter chains
each access and authz line to 313 as an `evidence_item`, on a host 215
does not control. Until then these lines are corroboration, not
evidence, and this document says so rather than letting a
`CRYPTOGRAPHICALLY-VERIFIED` banner imply otherwise.

## Four limits that survive a green verdict

1. **The pinned key came off the host being examined.** I read
   `chain-sign.pub` from 313 itself. `signer_trust PINNED` means the
   examiner supplied a key out of band relative to the *bundle* — it
   does not mean the key reached me through a channel independent of the
   evidence. A real examiner needs it from somewhere 313 cannot reach.
2. **`source_device_established: NO`**, in the verifier's own boundary
   results. Signatures prove what the signing key committed to, never
   which physical device produced it.
3. **The accounting key is a bearer credential.** A receipt proves
   possession of the shared secret, not physical origin — see
   `docs/TACACS-ACCOUNTING.md` §1. That key was exposed in an operator
   transcript on 2026-09-06 and is pending rotation, so receipts from
   before that rotation carry a secret with a known disclosure.
4. **The 2960's clock was wrong until 21:26:17 UTC.** It lost time in a
   power outage and was NTP-synced mid-session. Accounting records carry
   the *device's* clock, so anything timestamped before that boundary is
   not comparable with 313's and 215's clocks, which agree to within a
   second. Every record cited above post-dates the sync.
